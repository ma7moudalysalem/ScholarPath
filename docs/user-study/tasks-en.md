# Task scenarios

Read each scenario aloud **exactly as written**. A paraphrase is a hint.

Cap: 5 minutes per task. Record `completed`, `completed_with_help`, or `failed`,
the time in seconds, and one line on where the difficulty was.

The scenarios never name a menu, a button, or a page. Finding the right place is
part of what is being measured.

---

## T1 — Find an award

> You are finishing your bachelor's degree and want to do a master's abroad, in
> your own field, starting next year. Use this site to find a scholarship that
> looks worth applying for, and stop when you have one in front of you that you
> would consider.

**Success:** the participant has a single scholarship's detail page open and can
say why it is a candidate.

**Watch for:** whether they reach for search, for the filters, or for the
recommendations; whether they understand what the match score means.

---

## T2 — Judge whether you qualify

> Still on that scholarship — before spending an evening on the application, you
> want to know whether you actually qualify. Find out.

**Success:** the participant has run the eligibility check and can state, in
their own words, which requirements they meet and which they do not.

**Watch for:** whether a criterion reported as *partially met* is understood;
whether they treat the result as a decision or as advice.

---

## T3 — Get a document ready

> The application wants your transcript. You have it as a PDF on your computer.
> Put it where this site expects it, so it is ready to attach.

A dummy PDF named `transcript.pdf` is placed on the desktop before the session.

**Success:** the file is in the document vault and visible to the participant.

**Watch for:** whether they look for the upload inside the application form
instead of the vault, and whether they expect the file to be reusable.

---

## T4 — Apply

> Now apply for that scholarship, attaching the transcript you just uploaded.

**Success:** an application exists in a submitted state with the document
attached.

**Watch for:** whether they find the saved document or upload it a second time;
whether any required field is unclear; whether they hesitate before submitting.

---

## T5 — Find out what happened

> A week has passed. Come back and find out what has happened to that
> application, and whether anything is being asked of you.

The facilitator advances the test application to a state that needs the
participant's attention before the session; which state is recorded on the
session sheet.

**Success:** the participant locates the application, states its current status
correctly, and says what — if anything — they must do next.

**Watch for:** whether status names are read the way they are meant; whether the
timeline is noticed at all.

---

## T6 — Ask a question

> You are not sure whether this scholarship covers travel costs. Ask the site
> itself and see what it tells you.

**Success:** the participant uses the assistant and reaches an answer they can
judge.

**Watch for:** whether they find the assistant; whether they notice the sources
the answer cites; whether they believe the answer, and whether they say why.

---

## After the last task

Before the rating sheet, ask once, and write down the answer verbatim:

> Was there anything you expected to find and didn't?
